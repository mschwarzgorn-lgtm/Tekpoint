For a decade, one obstacle defied every robot vacuum on the market: the staircase. Thresholds, carpets, cables, and pets were all solved in turn, but stairs remained the boundary that no cleaning robot could cross. At CES 2026, Roborock crossed it.

## The World's First Stair-Climbing Robot Vacuum

The Saros Rover is the world's first robot vacuum built on an AI-powered wheel-leg architecture. It does not simply climb stairs — it cleans each step as it ascends, lifting and lowering each leg independently while holding its body level as the ground shifts beneath it. Curved staircases, carpeted steps, slopes, and multi-level thresholds fall inside its reach. The "no-go zone" that defined an entire product category is beginning to disappear.

## Discipline Behind the Breakthrough

The more telling story is the discipline behind the headline. The same CES 2026 launch introduced the Saros 20 and Saros 20 Sonic, delivering 35,000 Pa of suction, a sonic mop that scrubs 4,000 times per minute, and a chassis that learns and remembers how to navigate obstacles it has encountered before.

One product reaches for the moonshot while the others keep winning on the living-room floor today. That balance is what separates a gadget maker from a robotics company.

## From Cleaning Device to Household Robot

The category is moving from "cleaning device" to "household robot." The brands that treat mobility, autonomy, and real intelligence as a single system are the ones positioned to own the next decade of the connected home. The Saros Rover is a clear signal that the home robot is quietly becoming one of the most ambitious robotics platforms in any house.
