Every robot mower on the market makes a bet on one way of seeing the garden, and each of those bets carries a trade-off. Boundary wire is dependable but resents every landscaping change. RTK positioning removes the wire but depends on a clear view of the sky, which is harder to guarantee under dense trees. Camera-based navigation reads a garden much the way people do, and struggles when low sun washes out the image.

None of these approaches is wrong. Each simply has conditions in which it becomes less useful — and for a machine meant to work unattended, that is the whole question.

## Quad-Fusion Instead of a Single Method

ANTHBOT's M9 Pro went on sale across Europe on 27 July 2026, following a pre-order window from 15 to 26 July. Its answer to the trade-off is not to choose.

The M9 Pro combines four inputs — 360° LiDAR, RTK, NetRTK delivered over 4G, and dual HDR AI vision — in a system ANTHBOT calls HoloSense Quad-Fusion. Setup is wire-free, and where 4G NetRTK coverage reaches, there is no physical base station to mount either. For the installer and the end customer alike, that removes the two parts of robotic mowing that have always felt like construction work: burying wire and finding a spot for a station with a clear sky view.

The specification is aimed squarely at typical European gardens. The M9 Pro is rated for lawns up to 1,000 m², works at approximately 150 m² per hour, and handles slopes up to 45%, or about 24 degrees. Official European pricing is €1,299. A smaller M5 Pro sits below it at €999, with pre-order units shipping from 15 August.

## Why Sensor Fusion Is the Right Architecture

Sensor fusion is not a marketing term borrowed for the garden. It is the architecture that made autonomous vehicles credible.

Individually, every sensing method has a failure mode. Cameras struggle with glare and darkness. LiDAR struggles with reflective and transparent surfaces. Satellite positioning struggles with obstruction. Combining them means each weakness is covered by another input's strength, so performance degrades gradually rather than suddenly.

A garden is a far smaller environment than a motorway, but the logic transfers directly. Tree cover that weakens RTK does not affect LiDAR. Low sun that washes out vision does not affect satellite positioning. A flowerbed moved since the last mapping run is picked up live rather than assumed away. This is what separates a product that demonstrates well from one a household stops thinking about.

## The Investment That Isn't on the Spec Sheet

The detail that stands out most in this launch is not a sensor at all. ANTHBOT has established a logistics and service hub in Neuss, Germany.

For a Shenzhen-based manufacturer selling four-figure machines into European gardens, that is the unglamorous investment that determines whether a buyer becomes a repeat customer. Outdoor robotics live with weather, impacts and wear. Spare parts, warranty handling and turnaround times are not afterthoughts — they are the customer experience once the first season ends. A local hub is what makes those timelines realistic rather than aspirational.
